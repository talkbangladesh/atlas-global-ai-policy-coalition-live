## Source summary

I am an AI safety and governance (AIGP) researcher working at the intersection of technology, public interest, and global risks.

My professional background is rooted in infectious disease epidemiology, molecular biology and data science. Previously, I worked at the World Health Organization, where I delivered mission-critical analytics supporting outbreak response in multi-country deployments, enabling rapid institutional decision-making under uncertainty.  I led outbreak analytics during global health emergencies (eg. Go.Data software implementation for contact tracing for Ebola and COVID-19), and pandemic H1N1 influenza, Ebola, plague and COVID-19 outbreaks , across diverse contexts such as the Netherlands, the UK, Madagascar, Guinea, Mali, Laos PDR, Guatemala, the DRC, Fiji, and Moldova. This work required making high-stakes decisions under deep uncertainty, managing complex systems, and anticipating cascading failures, experiences that continue to shape how I approach AI risk.

As AI systems have become increasingly powerful and socially embedded, my focus has shifted toward AI safety, governance, and policy, particularly where failures could have large-scale or irreversible consequences. I work on issues including AI risk governance, algorithmic accountability and auditing, and the design of institutional and policy frameworks capable of responding to rapidly advancing technologies. I am currently working at the EU AI office on AI Safety. The European AI Office supports the development and adoption of trustworthy Artificial Intelligence (AI) solutions, while protecting people against risks.
