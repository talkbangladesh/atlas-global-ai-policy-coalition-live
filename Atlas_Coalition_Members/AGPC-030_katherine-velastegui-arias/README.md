## Source summary

I am an Ecuadorian lawyer working at the intersection of public law and the governance of automated decision-making inside public institutions. I hold an LL.M. in International Legal Studies from Georgetown University Law Center, which I completed as a Fulbright Scholar, and a Master's in International Litigation and Arbitration from Universidad San Francisco de Quito. For close to a decade I have worked on the State side of international dispute resolution and constitutional adjudication. At Ecuador's Attorney General's Office I defended the State in investment treaty and commercial arbitrations. At the Constitutional Court of Ecuador I drafted orders, opinions and judgments in constitutional control proceedings, and designed and validated the 128-rule detection set behind the Court's automated case-classification and prioritization system, validated against 40% of first-quarter 2026 case intake and built with an explicit human-review tier so that the routing decision remains human by design.
I works in Spanish and English and I'm interested in how justice-sector institutions in Latin America adopt AI under real capacity constraints.
